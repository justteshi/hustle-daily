Hustle Daily · product design v1

One day at a glance. Honest numbers. A calm close at 22:30.

Hustle Daily replaces the food, macro and energy spreadsheet. The iPhone collects Apple Health data and handles quick logging; the desktop web app is the full workspace for reviewing and editing the same data.

3 · Design principles

Missing is not zeroNo Apple Health data shows as “Missing”. Totals that depend on it say they can’t be calculated yet.
Every number shows its sourceEach value is labelled as Apple Health, calculated (=) or entered by you, so you know which ones you can edit.
Budget vs minimumCalories are a budget: “left” or “over”, never celebrated. Protein and steps are minimums: “to go” or “met ✓”.
Words beside colourDeficit and surplus are always written out. Status chips have a label and a shape. Red is only used for errors.
Calm, not coachingNo streaks, badges or verdicts. Plain, neutral wording such as “320 kcal left” or “Steps: 1,580 to go”.
Same model, right densityThe same day object everywhere. The desktop puts everything side by side with tables; the iPhone stacks it into cards.

5 · Assumptions recorded

  • A day runs from midnight to midnight in the user’s time zone (Europe/Sofia).
  • Sleep belongs to the day you wake up on.
  • Weight is the latest Apple Health reading of the day. If none arrives, it can be entered manually and is labelled “Entered by you”.
  • A logged entry keeps a copy of its nutrition. Editing a food later changes only future entries.
  • Energy values update during the day and are labelled “so far” until the day is concluded.
  • Open Food Facts lookup runs on the backend. The data is saved to your catalogue only after you confirm it.
  • Sign in uses email and password. There is no registration or social login, and an optional passkey can be added later.
  • Units are metric and energy is in kcal. Brand names in this prototype are fictional.
  • Strings sit in short, flexible containers so Bulgarian text (often 20–35% longer) has room.
  • Trends are full on the web; the iPhone shows a compact single-column version under More.
